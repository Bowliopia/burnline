Submissions

Send us a future worth tending.

Burnline is looking for solarpunk fiction and visual art rooted in character, ecological imagination, collective possibility, and the complicated work of building a more livable world.

Submission status

Open

We are currently accepting short fiction and visual art for future digital issues of Burnline.

Before you send

Choose a category and read the full guidelines.

We care more about the work than a polished pitch. The details below explain what Burnline is seeking, what files to send, and what contributors can expect.

Fiction

We are interested in solarpunk across a wide range of tones and settings: urban or rural, intimate or expansive, near-future or far beyond it. Above all, we care about character. Give us people who want something, risk something, and make us feel the stakes of the world they are trying to inhabit or change.

Genre fit Please submit work you would specifically describe as solarpunk or closely aligned ecological speculation.
01 · People

Character before concept

Strong ideas matter most when they shape recognizable lives, relationships, choices, and consequences.

02 · Systems

Futures with texture

Show us how communities live, organize, repair, distribute, grow, travel, care, or come into conflict.

03 · Possibility

Hope that has been earned

The world can remain difficult. We are drawn to stories that still leave room for agency and change.

Word limit
500–10,000 words.
Pay rate
Because Burnline is currently a free magazine without advertising, we cannot offer payment at this time. Our goal is to eventually pay a competitive per-word rate, but we are not there yet. We understand if that makes submission a non-starter for you.
How to submit
Email your story as a .doc, .docx, or .pdf to contact@burnlinemag.com. Include the word “Submission” in the subject line.

Human-created work only

No AI-generated or AI-assisted work will be accepted. Burnline is interested in fiction created by people.

Simultaneous submissions are welcome

Please submit elsewhere while you wait. Just let us know promptly if another publication accepts the story.

Visual art

We are accepting solarpunk-themed visual art for digital publication. Digital, hand-drawn, painted, collage, and mixed-media work are welcome. Photography is not currently being considered.

Selected work Artwork may appear in a digital issue and in Burnline’s online Art gallery, with full creator credit.
01 · Worlds

Places that feel inhabited

We welcome landscapes, interiors, infrastructure, neighborhoods, and everyday scenes shaped by ecological futures.

02 · People

Life within the future

Portraits, communities, rituals, labor, play, repair, and the relationships that make a world feel alive.

03 · Forms

Organic and unexpected

We are open to work that is bright, quiet, strange, imperfect, highly rendered, graphic, or experimental.

Artwork specs
Cover art should be portrait-oriented at 8.5 × 11 inches (2550 × 3300 px) and 300 dpi. Please have a PNG or high-quality JPG available if selected. Interior artwork may use any dimensions.
Pay rate
Because Burnline is currently a free magazine without advertising, we cannot offer payment at this time. Our goal is to eventually pay a competitive rate, but we are not there yet. We understand if that makes submission a non-starter for you.
How to submit
Email contact@burnlinemag.com with a link to the artwork wherever it is hosted, such as a portfolio or public post. Include the word “Submission” in the subject line.
Credit
Selected artists will be fully credited in the magazine and on the site using the name, portfolio link, and profile or social handles they would like included.

Human-created work only

No AI-generated or AI-assisted work will be accepted.

Send a link first

Please do not attach large artwork files to the initial email. A portfolio or hosted-image link is enough for review.

Cover letters

Keep it simple. The work comes first.

Emails and cover letters are much less important to Burnline than the fiction or artwork itself. If part of your personal story meaningfully informs the work, we would be glad to hear about it. Otherwise, a brief hello and the essential submission information are enough.

A good submission email Clear, brief, human.

Tell us what you are sending, include the requested file or link, and make sure “Submission” appears in the subject line.
