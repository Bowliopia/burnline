About Burnline

A home for futures worth imagining.

Burnline is an independent digital magazine for solarpunk fiction and art—stories about people adapting, repairing, organizing, sharing, and finding ways to build lives beyond the futures we have been warned about.

Why we are here

The future can hold more than collapse.

Burnline began as a place to gather the kind of short speculative fiction its editor wanted to read. That mission remains, but the lens has shifted toward stories that make room for possibility as well as conflict.

01 · What we publish

Hope with its sleeves rolled up.

We are interested in fiction and artwork shaped by ecological imagination, mutual aid, renewable abundance, public life, adaptation, and the difficult work of learning how to live together. The stories do not need to be utopias. They should simply believe that people can still make choices.

02 · What solarpunk means here

Not a spotless future—a living one.

Solarpunk is more than green architecture and bright skies. For Burnline, it is a way of asking who a future serves, who gets to shape it, what communities preserve, and what must be repaired. We welcome work that is hopeful, strange, grounded, unruly, and honest about the effort change requires.

03 · How it works

A small magazine, built deliberately.

Burnline is run mostly by a team of one, with help from contributors and collaborators along the way. Issues are designed as digital editions, while selected stories and artwork are also published individually so readers can encounter them in the format that suits them best.

Independent by design

Reading should not feel like navigating a billboard.

Burnline is—and is intended to remain—completely ad free. No pop-ups, sponsored interruptions, autoplaying distractions, or stories squeezed between marketing blocks. Readers should be able to open a piece, settle in, and read.

Keeping the magazine independent may eventually mean charging a modest amount for future editions. Any paid model will be designed around sustaining the publication and compensating the writers and artists whose work gives it a reason to exist. For now, Burnline remains free to read.

A standing promise

No ads. Ever.

The site and magazine will not be funded by cluttering the reading experience. Burnline would rather grow slowly and remain a place where the work comes first.
